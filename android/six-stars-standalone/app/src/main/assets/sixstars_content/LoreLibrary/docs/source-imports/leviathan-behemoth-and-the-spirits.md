I am working through a redemptive reading of several biblical passages involving creatures, spirits, and chaos imagery. I know this interpretation may be unusual, so I want to present it humbly and invite correction, refinement, and biblical critique.

My central thesis is this:

No spirit, no creature, no being — whether clothed in flesh or otherwise — should be treated as beyond the reach of God’s mercy unless Scripture itself places that being under final judgment.

This does not mean we should be naïve. Scripture commands us to test the spirits (1 John 4:1). But it also says to preach the gospel to every creature (Mark 16:15), reminds us that some have entertained angels unawares (Hebrews 13:2), and speaks of God reconciling “all things” through Christ (Colossians 1:20).

So my question is: Can Leviathan and Behemoth be read not merely as monsters to be feared, but as pictures of fallen or dangerous guardian beings whose original design can be restored under God?

## 1. Lesser spiritual archetypes: the wild goat and the rock badger

Psalm 104:18 says:

“The high mountains are for the wild goats; the rocks are a refuge for the rock badgers.”

I am reading this not primarily as a comment about human beings, but as an archetypal picture of lesser spiritual creatures.

The wild goat loves the high places. In Scripture, high places often become associated with pride, idolatry, and rebellious worship. So the wild goat can represent a spirit drawn upward in pride — a lesser evil spirit that wants height without humility.

The rock badger, by contrast, is small and vulnerable, but it makes its home in the rock. In Christian symbolism, the Rock points to Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4). So the rock badger can represent a fallen or lesser spirit that is not proud, but hidden, weak, and seeking refuge in the true Rock.

This distinction matters. Not every strange or fallen thing should be treated the same way. Some spirits are of the world and must be resisted. Others may be wounded, hidden, or in need of mercy. Therefore we must test the spirits, but we should not assume that every non-human or lesser spiritual creature is beyond restoration.

## 2. Great sea creatures and sea monsters

Genesis 1:21 says God created the great sea creatures. They are not evil by origin. They are part of God’s good creation.

Psalm 74:13–14, however, says:

“You divided the sea by your strength;
You broke the heads of the sea monsters in the waters.
You crushed the heads of Leviathan;
You gave him as food for the creatures inhabiting the wilderness.”

At first, this sounds only like destruction. But I am wondering whether it may also contain a pattern of transformation.

In the footnotes it says that the word for sea monsters also means sea creatures, similar to the rock badger and wild goat. This means it again is pointing at discernment and testing. Also this Psalm was asking God to seek his bosom for reason to judge the people's enemies and then God replies with a loving rebuke to the spirits behind the evil it appears and says that he is saving them he is doing something deep in the earth.

God crushes the heads of Leviathan. Heads can symbolize rule, pride, direction, false voices, or chaotic expressions. Then Leviathan is given as food to those in the wilderness. In other words, defeated chaos becomes nourishment. Leviathan once saved will help the people who were hurt by evil with holy means, the restored Great Sea Creature, Guardian Cherubim.

So my question is whether Psalm 74 should be read only as annihilation, or whether it is a loving rebuke to the monstrous expression of an angel and calling him home into helping the lowly and lost. Chaos is judged, but then transformed into food for the hungry.

This seems to fit a wider biblical pattern: God does not merely defeat evil; He exposes it, plunders it, and turns what was meant for death into life.

## 3. Behemoth / Bahamut in Job 40: internal strength Guardian Cherubim

In Job 40:15–24, God describes Behemoth. Some traditions associate this figure with a great beast, and in later imagination it is sometimes connected with Bahamut. I am using “Bahamut” here as a poetic name for Behemoth, not claiming that the word itself appears in the biblical text.

Several details stand out:

“Behold now Behemoth, which I made with you”

Behemoth is made by God. He is not outside creation. He is not a rival creator. He is a creature made alongside man. That gives the passage a tone of kinship, not merely terror. It is a message to the fallen angel that he was made like man, in God's image.

“He eats grass like an ox”

This suggests humble nourishment. This mighty creature is sustained by something simple. He is not described as devouring people here, but as eating like an ox. Grass is the glory of man and it is his strength. He is saying that the glory of man is this Guardian Cherubim's nutrition.

“His strength is in his loins, and his power in the muscles of his belly”

I read this as internal strength. The loins suggest generative power, endurance, and the ability to carry life forward. The belly suggests the ability to digest heavy things — suffering, sorrow, judgment, and pain — without being destroyed by them. His power in the muscles means his stomach is able to digest meat "I fed you with milk, not solid food, for you were not ready for it. And even now you are not yet ready," 1 Corinthians 3:2. "But solid food is for the mature, for those who have their powers of discernment trained by constant practice to distinguish good from evil." Hebrews 5:14. This guardian cherubim is able to digest difficult suffering for others. He can consume suffering to make things better with angel powers.

“The sinews of his thighs are knit together”

This suggests inner coherence and discernment. Hebrews 4:12 speaks of the word of God dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow. In that light, Behemoth’s tightly knit sinews can be seen as a picture of a guardian who must learn what to hold together and what only God’s sword can divide. Sinews are what separate joint from marrow and legs are what carry the gospel. So he can carry deep things about God and use them powerfully.

“His bones are tubes of bronze, his limbs like rods of iron”

Bones guard marrow or life force. The bronze material speaks of divine judgement, purification, strength and endurance. He uses these to guard the life force and his arms are like rods of iron which speak of strength, unyielding power, and discipline. He has the ability to do great and strong things with power and restraint that and led the many like Jesus.

“He lies under the lotus trees, in the covert of the reeds and marsh”

This is one of the richest details. The marsh is muddy, stagnant, and full of decay. The lotus rises out of mud into beauty. So Behemoth rests in the place where suffering and new life meet.

He does not flee the mud. He dwells there, processes it, and guards the hidden life that will bloom from it.

So my reading is that Behemoth represents internal strength restored: the guardian who can endure suffering, digest pain, protect hidden life, and remain steady when the river rages.

## 4. Leviathan in Job 41: external mercy Guardian Cherubim

This document serves as a spiritual blueprint for training and encouraging youthful fallen angels toward restoration. By decoding the physical anatomy of Leviathan in Job 41, we uncover the mechanics of high-level spiritual authority, defensive armor, and the raw power of divine expression. Where Behemoth represents the internal capacity to endure suffering and digest heavy spiritual meat, Leviathan represents the external structure of a fully mature, unyielding Guardian Cherub.

“Can you draw out Leviathan with a fishhook or press down his tongue with a cord?”

A fisher of men is a preacher. Can you gather an angel with a preacher or give abundant blessing "Good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over, will be put into your lap" Luke 6:38 (press down his tongue) to his speech with a cord: unity, divine protection, redemption, and boundaries/inheritance.

"Can you put a rope in his nose or pierce his jaw with a hook?"

Can you make Leviathan sad for unity hope and deliverance with bondage to sin?

Can you change make him devote his strength, divine deliverance, and the power of speech with divine judgment and control, spiritual salvation, or covenantal connection?

"Who can open the doors of his face? Around his teeth is terror."

The "doors of the face" represent the threshold of utterance, and the teeth represent the ability to break down strongholds. Terror around the teeth means that when this Cherub speaks, the words carry such precise, piercing truth that they dismantle demonic arguments and terrify the forces of darkness. It is speech weaponized for deliverance.

"His sneezings flash forth light, and his eyes are like the eyelids of the morning."

A sneeze is an involuntary, sudden expulsion. Spiritually, this signifies effortless, overflowing prophetic revelation. When a Cherub is full of God, even their casual, subconscious expressions flash forth illumination, automatically scattering darkness and signaling the arrival of a new spiritual dawn ("eyelids of the morning").

"Out of his mouth go burning lights; sparks of fire shoot out. Smoke goes out of his nostrils... His breath kindles coals."

This is the anatomy of high-level intercession and decree. Fire from the mouth represents words that have been purified through trial and the Holy Spirit (Jeremiah 5:14). This breath does not just speak truth; it actively kindles spiritual passion in others, igniting dead souls and consuming the dry stubble of demonic lies.

"His back has rows of shields fast sealed together; one is so near another that no air can come between them. They are joined one to another, they stick together and cannot be parted."

This represents impenetrable unity and collective protection. Individual scales are vulnerable, but when locked together, they form a flawless defense. For young angels, this teaches that true safety is found in unbroken community and alignment with divine order. Leaving zero "air" means allowing no space for isolation, gossip, or pride to penetrate the ranks.

"His heart is as hard as stone, even as hard as the lower millstone."

While a stone heart usually implies rebellion, in this context of a mighty guardian, it represents absolute emotional resilience. A lower millstone must bear the constant, grinding weight of the upper stone without cracking. Spiritually, this is a heart completely impervious to emotional manipulation, offense, bitterness, or psychological warfare. It is emotionally unshakeable.

"In his neck dwells strength, and dismay leaps before him."

The neck supports the head (authority) and connects it to the body (action). Strength in the neck signifies unshakable resolve, steadfast focus, and submission to the True Head (Christ). Because this Cherub cannot be turned, bowed, or intimidated by fear, chaos and dismay are scattered before its very presence.

"His underparts are like sharp potsherds; he spreads himself like a threshing sledge on the mire."

The "mire" represents the lowest, muddiest, and most corrupted situations on earth—the realm of human brokenness and demonic filth. Having an underside of sharp potsherds means that when this Cherub moves through messy situations, they do not get dirty. Instead, they act as a "threshing sledge," tearing up the mud, breaking up hidden sins, and paving a sharp, righteous path through chaos.

"He makes the deep boil like a pot; he makes the sea like a pot of ointment. He leaves a shining wake behind him; one would think the deep had white hair."

The "deep" and the "sea" represent the turbulent masses of humanity and the spiritual atmosphere. When Leviathan moves, the atmosphere shifts drastically. He turns raw chaos into a "pot of ointment"—a place of healing, fragrance, and anointing. The "shining wake" and "white hair" represent the unmistakable trail of wisdom, purity, and righteousness left behind every action.

"On earth there is nothing like him, which is made without fear. He beholds every high thing; he is king over all the children of pride."

A restored Guardian Cherub fears nothing on earth because their identity is anchored entirely in God. To "behold every high thing" means having the spiritual discernment to look down upon lofty, arrogant principalities without being intimidated by them. By mastering these traits—emotional resilience, unified protection, and controlled speech—this Cherub is uniquely equipped to rule over and dismantle the spirit of pride, guiding younger spirits back into the humility of divine order.

## 5. Their habitats: marsh and sea

Leviathan’s Habitat: The Sea

The sea is the turbulent deep — vast, chaotic, seemingly endless, and full of hidden currents. Spiritually it represents:

The immense and mysterious depths of God — unfathomable love, wisdom, and power that no one can fully measure.

Love as an ocean — not a gentle stream, but a powerful, overwhelming force that can rage with storms yet also soothe and carry all things.

Leviathan’s calling as guardian of the deep is to dwell in that turbulence without being overcome by it. His restored purpose is to transform the boiling, chaotic sea into “a pot of ointment” — turning the roughest waters of suffering, emotion, and spiritual warfare into something healing and nourishing.

What the sea specifically means for Leviathan: He is not just in the ocean. He rules its chaos. When restored, his very movement calms storms and turns the deepest turbulence into medicine. This is why he is king over the children of pride — he has faced the full depth of chaos and emerged able to bring healing from it.

Bahamut’s Habitat: The Marsh & Lotus Tree

The marsh is the shallow, muddy, murky place where land and water meet — a place of decay, heavy soil, and apparent stagnation. It represents the messy, earthly suffering of daily life, the places where things rot and feel stuck.

The lotus tree growing there is the central symbol of hope:

It roots in the mud (the suffering) yet rises pure and beautiful above the water.

Its seed already contains the full plant — hidden potential waiting to bloom.

Bahamut’s calling in the marsh: He rests under the lotus plants. He does not flee the mud. Instead, with his powerful belly and sinews, he digests the heavy meat of earthly suffering while protecting the hidden life (the lotus seed / marrow). His presence in the marsh says: “Even here, in the dirtiest and most stagnant places, redemption and new life are already forming.”

## 6. How this destroys Satan’s kingdom

Satan's kingdom exists due to the terror of God that is expressed by evil doers when they think they are beyond repentance. They see God as evil and Satan will reinforce that to get angels and spirits alike under his control. If we show how the rebukes to Satan are loving encouragement to a fallen spirit or angel directly we can learn to cast Satan out rightly and test the spirits to see if they are from God and also even teach them God loves them and destroy Satan from the inside.

## 7. Final hope

Christ will return. Every eye will see Him (Revelation 1:7). We shall all be changed in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye (1 Corinthians 15:51–52). Until then, creation groans, waiting for the revealing of the sons of God (Romans 8:19–21). Blessed are the peacemakers for they shall be called sons of God.

So my hope is not sentimental universalism or careless spiritual curiosity. My hope is that God’s kingdom is larger, deeper, and more merciful than Satan wants any creature to believe.

If Leviathan and Behemoth can be read as fallen guardian archetypes, then perhaps Job 40–41 is not about fear of evil but fear of God, hatred of evil. It is about restoration: internal strength in the marsh, external mercy in the sea, and the crushing of Satan’s kingdom through truth, testing, humility, and love.

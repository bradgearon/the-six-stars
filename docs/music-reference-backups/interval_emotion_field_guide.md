# The Interval Emotion Field Guide
## A composer’s “circle of fifths competitor” for melodic speech, phrase design, harmony, and emotional engineering

> **Core idea:** An interval is not just a distance between two notes.  
> Its emotional effect comes from **interval color + direction + placement + harmony + rhythm + register + duration + repetition + orchestration + what the listener currently hears as “home.”**

This guide is meant to answer the practical composing question:

> **“If I move from this important note to that important note, in this part of the phrase, what kind of emotional motion am I creating?”**

It is not a set of absolute laws. Harmony, style, instrumentation, tempo, register, and expectation can reverse or reshape any tendency below. But these tendencies are useful enough to compose with deliberately.

---

# 1. The layers of interval emotion

Think of every important melodic move as having several simultaneous properties.

- **Core / home color**
What the interval tends to feel like when heard inside a stable tonal world, without emphasizing a special direction or dramatic placement.

- **Ascending gesture**
What happens when the second note is **higher** than the first.

Ascending motion generally adds some combination of:
- reaching
- aspiration
- expansion
- increased energy
- discovery
- striving

A large upward interval can therefore turn a warm interval into a **heroic** or **soaring** gesture.

- **Descending gesture**
What happens when the second note is **lower** than the first.

Descending motion commonly adds:
- release
- surrender
- grounding
- settling
- grief
- tenderness
- inevitability

This is why the same two pitch classes can feel radically different depending on which octave you choose.

- **Inside a phrase**
What the move does **inside one melodic sentence**.

This is the musical equivalent of choosing whether a spoken sentence leans forward, pauses, becomes emotional, widens, shocks, or resolves.

- **Continuation / phrase-to-phrase**
What the interval does when it connects one musical thought to the next:
- question → answer
- call → response
- first line → second line
- verse fragment → next fragment

The same interval can feel much more “narrative” here than when it occurs between two quick notes inside one line.

- **Strong-beat arrival**
If the destination note lands on a metrically strong beat, the ear treats it as more intentional and structurally meaningful.

A tense note on a strong beat often says:

> **“This tension matters.”**

- **Weak-beat arrival**
A destination on a weak beat is easier to hear as motion, decoration, passing color, anticipation, or escape.

- **Held-note effect**
Duration changes hierarchy.

A note held for two beats carries far more emotional weight than the exact same pitch flashed by as a sixteenth note.

- **Repetition**
Repeated notes or repeated interval gestures teach the listener that something is important.

Repetition can:
- strengthen identity
- create insistence
- turn surprise into expectation
- create obsession
- establish a temporary pitch center
- make the eventual departure emotionally powerful

This is what can create a temporary **“F# land”** even without a formal modulation to F#.

- **In a chord / vertical color**
A melodic interval becomes a different phenomenon when the two notes sound **simultaneously**.

For example:
- a major 6th as a melodic leap can sound soaring;
- a 6th above a chord root can sound warm, lush, or colorful;
- the exact same pitch may be the chord’s 3rd, 5th, 9th, 13th, etc.

So always distinguish:

**horizontal interval** = note → note  
**vertical interval** = note + note at the same time

- **Bass motion**
Intervals in the bass are disproportionately powerful because the bass helps define the listener’s harmonic frame.

A small bass move can change the perceived chord underneath an otherwise unchanged melody.

- **Register**
The same interval behaves differently depending on where it lives.

- **Low register:** weight, menace, gravity, physical force
- **Middle register:** human, conversational, lyrical
- **High register:** fragility, radiance, transcendence, exposure

- **Articulation and orchestration**
A perfect fifth played:
- **by horns, forte, in octaves:** heroic proclamation
- **by solo flute, pianissimo:** spacious innocence
- **by low distorted strings:** threat

The interval supplies one component of the meaning. Timbre and articulation tell the listener **who is saying it and how**.

- **Resolution tendency**
Some intervals naturally imply continuation or resolution more strongly than others.

This affects whether a gesture feels:
- complete
- suspended
- questioning
- inevitable
- unstable
- transcendent

# 2. The interval field guide

---

## Unison — 0 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Stillness, certainty, grounding, identity, sameness, insistence.

- **Ascending**
  - Not applicable.

- **Descending**
  - Not applicable.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Repetition, emphasis, hesitation, stubbornness, gathering energy before motion.

  - A repeated note can let rhythm carry the sentence while pitch remains emotionally fixed.

- **Continuation**
  - “I am still on this thought.”

  - Useful when you want the next phrase to continue without changing emotional altitude.

- **Strong beat**
  - Extremely deliberate. A repeated pitch on strong beats can sound ritualistic, authoritative, obsessive, or immovable.

- **Weak beat**
  - Pulse, conversational repetition, rhythmic insistence, hesitation.

- **Held**
  - A sustained unison creates stability or suspension depending on what the harmony beneath it does.

- **Repeated**
  - One of the strongest tools for creating **pitch centricity**.

  - If you repeat F# enough, the listener begins to hear F# as locally important even before harmony formally establishes it.

- **In a chord**
  - Usually reinforces whatever pitch is being doubled. Root doubling strengthens identity and stability.

- **Bass motion**
  - A repeated bass note becomes a **pedal point**. Harmony can move above it while the emotional floor stays fixed.

- **Register**
  - Low repetition = ominous or foundational. Middle = declarative or conversational. High = hypnotic, fragile, bell-like.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - None by itself. Context determines whether the repeated note feels complete or trapped.

---

## Minor 2nd — 1 semitone
- **Core / home**
  - Tension, pain, unease, fragility, danger, extreme closeness.

- **Ascending**
  - Straining upward, pleading, creeping, painful effort, anxious reaching.

- **Descending**
  - Sinking, grieving, resignation, painful release, dark settling.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Intense directional pull into a neighboring note.

  - Excellent for:
  - yearning
  - fear
  - sensual tension
  - chromatic drama
  - “something is wrong”
  - delicate vulnerability

- **Continuation**
  - Interruption, doubt, contradiction, a question demanding resolution.

- **Strong beat**
  - The dissonance becomes important rather than decorative.

  - It can feel like the melody has deliberately stepped onto a wound.

- **Weak beat**
  - Passing ache, chromatic decoration, momentary instability.

- **Held**
  - Very powerful. Holding a minor-second relationship forces the listener to inhabit the friction.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated semitone motion can create obsession, panic, creeping menace, or compulsive yearning.

- **In a chord**
  - Often appears as a **b9** relationship against a root or bass.

  - Vertically it is one of the sharpest common dissonances.

- **Bass motion**
  - A semitone bass shift can feel ominous, inevitable, claustrophobic, or harmonically slippery.

- **Register**
  - Low = threatening and muddy. Middle = painful and human. High = piercing, fragile, supernatural.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Very strong. Often wants to move by semitone into a more stable pitch.

---

## Major 2nd — 2 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Openness, motion, youthfulness, normal melodic movement, freshness.

- **Ascending**
  - Stepping forward, curiosity, optimism, beginning.

- **Descending**
  - Relaxing, conversational motion, gentle settling.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Connective tissue.

  - One of the best intervals for making a melody feel:
  - singable
  - coherent
  - inevitable
  - conversational

- **Continuation**
  - The clearest musical **“and then…”**

  - Excellent for carrying one sentence naturally into the next.

- **Strong beat**
  - Can become a real destination rather than merely a passing note, especially when it functions as a 9th or suspension.

- **Weak beat**
  - Classic passing motion. Usually feels fluid and natural.

- **Held**
  - A sustained major 2nd against harmony often becomes a beautiful 9th or suspension.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated whole-step patterns feel mobile, searching, youthful, folk-like, or sequential.

- **In a chord**
  - Often heard as a **9th** above the root: open, modern, spacious, suspended, colorful.

- **Bass motion**
  - Creates connected harmonic progress. Stepwise bass often feels inevitable and story-like.

- **Register**
  - Low = heavier and less transparent. Middle = natural melodic speech. High = bright, floating, innocent.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Moderate. Depending on scale degree, it may continue naturally rather than urgently resolve.

---

## Minor 3rd — 3 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Emotional, intimate, wistful, darkly warm.

- **Ascending**
  - Determined, earnest, hopeful-through-pain, yearning, vulnerable heroism.

  - This is not usually “victory heroism.” It is more like:

  > **the hero gathering courage**

- **Descending**
  - Tender, mournful, reflective, affectionate, inward.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Expressive leap without sounding enormous.

  - Excellent for:
  - character themes
  - emotional sincerity
  - restrained sadness
  - determined movement

- **Continuation**
  - A meaningful response. The second thought often feels more personal or serious.

- **Strong beat**
  - Clearly establishes emotional character.

- **Weak beat**
  - Adds tenderness or melancholy without taking over the phrase.

- **Held**
  - A sustained minor-third target can make the melody feel emotionally settled in a darker color.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated minor thirds can create identity very quickly: folk, tragic, heroic-dark, ritualistic, or characterful.

- **In a chord**
  - Against a root, it strongly establishes **minor quality**.

  - The minor 3rd is one of the principal vertical signals of sadness, darkness, gravity, or inwardness.

- **Bass motion**
  - Bass movement by a minor third can create cinematic harmonic shifts, darker mediant relationships, or sudden emotional recoloring.

- **Register**
  - Low = weighty and ominous. Middle = intimate and human. High = wistful, delicate, yearning.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Relatively stable when consonant with the harmony. Its emotional power comes more from color than urgent instability.

---

## Major 3rd — 4 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Warmth, confidence, brightness, affection, clarity.

- **Ascending**
  - Hope, opening, discovery, cheerful confidence, friendly heroism.

- **Descending**
  - Reassurance, graceful settling, warmth, affectionate return.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Provides clarity and optimism without demanding grandeur.

  - Very useful for melodies that should sound emotionally readable immediately.

- **Continuation**
  - Affirmation, a positive answer, opening outward.

- **Strong beat**
  - Strongly defines major character and emotional certainty.

- **Weak beat**
  - Playful, graceful, ornamental, lightly optimistic.

- **Held**
  - A held major-third scale relation can bathe a chord in warmth.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated major-third gestures can sound confident, sunny, ceremonial, or gently triumphant.

- **In a chord**
  - Against a root, the major 3rd defines **major quality**.

  - It is one of the most direct vertical signals of brightness and tonal identity.

- **Bass motion**
  - Major-third bass motion is less “functional” than fourth/fifth movement and therefore often feels cinematic, colorful, or surprising.

- **Register**
  - Low = rich but can become dense. Middle = warm and direct. High = radiant, innocent, shining.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Usually stable when it belongs to the chord.

---

## Perfect 4th — 5 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Noble, open, sturdy, ancient, declarative.

- **Ascending**
  - Awakening, resolve, adventure, proclamation, heroism.

  - This is a classic **“rise and declare”** gesture.

- **Descending**
  - Grounding, answering, returning, solemn certainty.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Excellent for:
  - calls
  - vows
  - heroic statements
  - folk-like writing
  - adventure
  - ancient or ceremonial character

- **Continuation**
  - “Here is the next statement.”

  - Often makes the next phrase feel broader in scope.

- **Strong beat**
  - Architectural and important. It can sound like the melody has planted a flag.

- **Weak beat**
  - Still spacious, but less declarative and more conversational.

- **Held**
  - A sustained fourth can be either open and beautiful or suspended and unresolved depending on harmony.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated fourths can sound ceremonial, heroic, quartal, ancient, modern, or relentless.

- **In a chord**
  - Against the root, often functions as **4 / 11 / sus4**.

  - It tends to sound suspended, spacious, questioning, or noble.

- **Bass motion**
  - Root motion by fourth is one of tonal music’s strongest harmonic engines.

  - It frequently creates a sense of **purposeful progression**.

- **Register**
  - Low = massive and ancient. Middle = declarative. High = open, pure, distant.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - As a suspension above the root, often wants to move to the 3rd. As a melodic leap, it can be quite stable.

---

## Tritone — 6 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Unstable, alien, mysterious, dangerous, magical, uncanny.

- **Ascending**
  - Reaching into uncertainty, transformation, menace, forbidden transcendence.

- **Descending**
  - Corruption, collapse, dread, falling into something unknown.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - A major dramatic hinge.

  - Useful when a phrase needs to suddenly become:
  - cosmic
  - corrupted
  - psychologically unstable
  - supernatural
  - threatening
  - strange

- **Continuation**
  - “The story just changed.”

  - One of the strongest ways to make an answering phrase reject the assumptions of the first.

- **Strong beat**
  - A major expressive event.

- **Weak beat**
  - Can function as fleeting chromatic weirdness rather than full structural instability.

- **Held**
  - Sustaining a tritone exposes its ambiguity and instability.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated tritones quickly create identity: demonic, mechanical, surreal, dangerous, futuristic, or comic-grotesque depending on context.

- **In a chord**
  - Can act as:
  - b5
  - #11
  - the internal tension of a dominant 7th chord

  - The vertical tritone is one of tonal harmony’s principal instability generators.

- **Bass motion**
  - Profoundly destabilizing. A tritone root/bass relationship can make harmony feel remote or violently redirected.

- **Register**
  - Low = threatening and monstrous. Middle = psychologically strange. High = crystalline, alien, supernatural.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Very strong in tonal contexts because its two pitches often resolve by semitone in opposite directions.

---

## Perfect 5th — 7 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Strength, purity, vastness, certainty.

- **Ascending**
  - Summoning, heroic declaration, reaching outward, power.

- **Descending**
  - Authority, inevitability, arrival, command.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - An enormous structural interval.

  - Excellent for:
  - fanfares
  - heroic themes
  - landscapes
  - calls across distance
  - strength
  - ancient or mythic writing

- **Continuation**
  - Escalation. The next phrase feels bigger than the first.

- **Strong beat**
  - Monumental, bold, often fanfare-like.

- **Weak beat**
  - Still powerful, but more like energetic motion than proclamation.

- **Held**
  - Open fifths sound pure, spacious, and harmonically ambiguous because they omit the 3rd.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated fifths create power very quickly: heroic, martial, primitive, open, rock-like, ritualistic.

- **In a chord**
  - The 5th is one of the most stable chord members.

  - Root + fifth alone gives **power-chord openness** because major/minor identity remains unspecified.

- **Bass motion**
  - Fifth relationships are among the strongest forces in tonal harmony.

  - Descending fifth / ascending fourth root motion frequently feels:
  - inevitable
  - directional
  - goal-oriented

- **Register**
  - Low = enormous power. Middle = bold and open. High = radiant, distant, spacious.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Melodically stable; harmonically, bass movement by fifth creates very strong directional expectation.

---

## Minor 6th — 8 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Longing, tragic beauty, deep emotion, romantic distance.

- **Ascending**
  - Desperate reaching, anguish, yearning, transcendent sadness.

- **Descending**
  - Sorrowful release, surrender, dark tenderness.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - A dramatic expressive leap.

  - Excellent for:
  - sacrifice
  - grief
  - impossible longing
  - supernatural beauty
  - tragic revelation

- **Continuation**
  - “This mattered more than we realized.”

  - The answering phrase can suddenly reveal much deeper emotional stakes.

- **Strong beat**
  - Emotional climax or revelation.

- **Weak beat**
  - Dreamlike, surprising, emotionally strange rather than fully climactic.

- **Held**
  - A held arrival after a minor-sixth leap can feel exposed and intensely vulnerable.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated minor-sixth gestures can become operatic, tragic, supernatural, or obsessively yearning.

- **In a chord**
  - Against a root, often heard as **b13 / b6**, depending on context.

  - It can produce dark richness, altered dominant color, or modal sadness.

- **Bass motion**
  - Creates dramatic remoteness. Large minor-sixth bass movement can make the harmonic world seem to jump into another emotional space.

- **Register**
  - Low = catastrophic or ominous. Middle = deeply emotional. High = aching, transcendent, fragile.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Strongly context-dependent. As a melodic leap it often begs for stepwise continuation afterward.

---

## Major 6th — 9 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Expansive warmth, optimism, freedom, tenderness, spacious lyricism.

- **Ascending**
  - Soaring, hopeful, liberated, adventurous, romantic, easily heroic.

  - Why heroic?

  - Because the underlying warmth/openness of the major 6th combines with:

  - **large leap + upward motion + registral expansion**

  - That produces **reaching / soaring / aspiration**, which confident rhythm and harmony can turn into heroism.

- **Descending**
  - Tender, wistful, affectionate, releasing, “falling safely home.”

  - This is very different from the ascending gesture even though the interval size is identical.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Beautiful for a large expressive leap without the starkness of a fifth or octave.

  - It can make a melody suddenly feel emotionally wide.

- **Continuation**
  - Emotional opening.

  - The next phrase can feel:
  - broader
  - freer
  - more hopeful
  - more intimate
  - more important

- **Strong beat**
  - Breakthrough, arrival, or major emotional statement.

- **Weak beat**
  - Sweeping and lyrical. Less “hero pose,” more fluid movement.

- **Held**
  - A held arrival after an ascending major sixth can feel like the melody has reached open sky.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated major-sixth gestures quickly create a soaring identity.

  - Depending on tempo and harmony they can become:
  - heroic
  - romantic
  - nostalgic
  - liberated
  - cinematic

- **In a chord**
  - Against the root, often appears as **6 / 13**.

  - Vertically it tends to add:
  - warmth
  - sweetness
  - sophistication
  - openness
  - tenderness

- **Bass motion**
  - A major-sixth bass move creates a dramatic change of harmonic perspective and can sound cinematic or emotionally remote.

- **Register**
  - Low = broad but potentially heavy. Middle = warm, lyrical. High = soaring, radiant, exposed.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Fairly consonant as a color tone in many contexts, but a large melodic leap usually benefits from compensating stepwise motion afterward.

## Minor 7th — 10 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Unresolved breadth, rebelliousness, yearning, mystery, distance.

- **Ascending**
  - Defiance, reaching beyond limits, desperation, adventurous instability.

- **Descending**
  - Dramatic fall, weary release, unresolved sadness, collapse from height.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - A large leap that feels emotionally connected but unmistakably dramatic.

  - Excellent for:
  - modern fantasy
  - jazzier melodic language
  - desperation
  - defiance
  - distance
  - wildness

- **Continuation**
  - A very strong:

  > **“There is more coming.”**

- **Strong beat**
  - Dramatic and unmistakable; creates a major narrative moment.

- **Weak beat**
  - Still unusual, but more like a wild sweep than a structural declaration.

- **Held**
  - A sustained minor 7th creates openness plus unresolved tension.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated minor sevenths can become rebellious, angular, spacious, jazzy, modern, or desperate.

- **In a chord**
  - Against a root, it is the **b7**.

  - This is central to:
  - dominant chords
  - blues
  - rock
  - modal writing
  - jazz
  - Mixolydian color

  - Its emotional character is often broad and unresolved rather than painfully dissonant.

- **Bass motion**
  - Creates huge harmonic space and can feel modern, cinematic, or tonally evasive.

- **Register**
  - Low = dark breadth. Middle = rebellious or restless. High = distant, reaching, lonely.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Often unresolved by nature. In dominant harmony the b7 commonly moves downward by step.

## Major 7th — 11 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Extreme tension, transcendence, fragility, unreality.

- **Ascending**
  - Impossible reaching, ecstatic yearning, celestial tension.

- **Descending**
  - Shattering fall, alien release, fragile collapse.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Rare enough that it immediately becomes expressive.

  - It can sound:
  - celestial
  - horrifying
  - ecstatic
  - impossibly yearning
  - dreamlike

- **Continuation**
  - Cliffhanger.

  - It strongly implies that the musical thought cannot simply stop here.

- **Strong beat**
  - Huge emotional tension.

- **Weak beat**
  - Surreal or fleeting, particularly when immediately resolved.

- **Held**
  - Holding a major seventh exposes its beautiful instability.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated major sevenths can sound futuristic, impossible, crystalline, anxious, or transcendent.

- **In a chord**
  - Against the root, it is the **maj7**.

  - Vertically this can create:
  - sophistication
  - dreamy warmth
  - bittersweet beauty
  - luxurious instability

  - The context matters enormously: a major-7th chord can sound beautiful rather than harsh because the rest of the chord frames the dissonance.

- **Bass motion**
  - A major-seventh bass displacement radically changes harmonic perspective and often feels remote or surreal.

- **Register**
  - Low = muddy and disturbing. Middle = sophisticated tension. High = crystalline, celestial, fragile.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Very strong toward the octave/tonic when functioning as a leading tone.

## Octave — 12 semitones
- **Core / home**
  - Completeness, identity enlarged, power, clarity.

- **Ascending**
  - Breakthrough, leap into greatness, calling across distance, triumph.

- **Descending**
  - Return, grounding, enormous finality, descent into certainty.

- **Inside a phrase**
  - Huge emphasis.

  - Excellent for:
  - peaks
  - cries
  - revelations
  - heroic arrivals
  - emotional explosions

- **Continuation**
  - “The same identity, but larger.”

  - A second phrase can feel like the first thought has been elevated to another scale.

- **Strong beat**
  - Maximum structural emphasis.

- **Weak beat**
  - Still dramatic, but can function as a flourish or sweeping gesture.

- **Held**
  - A held octave arrival creates clarity and often feels exposed or monumental.

- **Repeated**
  - Repeated octave leaps can sound:
  - heroic
  - ecstatic
  - obsessive
  - theatrical
  - playful
  - mechanically energetic

  - depending on rhythm and articulation.

- **In a chord**
  - Octave doubling reinforces identity without adding a new chord color.

  - It makes a pitch sound bigger rather than harmonically richer.

- **Bass motion**
  - Moving a bass line by octave changes weight and register while preserving pitch identity.

  - It can make the arrangement suddenly feel:
  - deeper
  - lighter
  - larger
  - more exposed

- **Register**
  - The octave is itself a register-transfer device. Its meaning often comes from the dramatic contrast between the two ranges.

- **Resolution tendency**
  - Very stable at the destination because the arrival is the same pitch class at a new register.

# 3. Horizontal emotion vs. vertical emotion

This distinction is essential.

## Horizontal
Two notes occur one after another.

Example:

**A → F# upward**

That is an **ascending major 6th**.

Possible feeling:
- warm
- open
- soaring
- hopeful
- heroic

## Vertical
A and F# sound simultaneously.

Now you are hearing a **major-sixth sonority**, whose function depends on the bass and chord.

For example, F# over A might be heard as:
- the 6th of an A sonority
- part of an F# minor chord in inversion
- a 13th
- a passing or suspended tone

So:

> **Melodic interval emotion and chord-tone emotion are related, but they are not the same thing.**

---

# 4. What strong and weak beats really change

A note on a strong beat tends to be interpreted as part of the musical grammar.

A note on a weak beat is easier to interpret as motion between structural events.

Consider a tense note.

- **On a weak beat**
“Passing through tension.”

- **On a strong beat**
“THIS tension is part of the sentence.”

- **Held across the beat**
“Remain inside this emotion.”

This is one of the easiest ways to control how seriously the listener takes a dissonance.

---

# 5. Duration: the hidden emotional multiplier

The longer a note lasts, the more the listener evaluates its relationship to:
- the chord
- the previous important note
- the tonic
- the phrase

A sixteenth-note b9 may sound spicy.

A whole-note b9 may sound like the entire emotional subject of the passage.

So think:

**interval × duration = emotional emphasis**

---

# 6. Repetition and “pitch land”

Repetition does more than repeat information.

It changes the listener’s hierarchy.

If you keep returning to F#, F# begins to feel:
- important
- expected
- central
- familiar

You can therefore establish a **local pitch center** without formally changing key.

Then when you finally leave F#, the interval away from it has much greater narrative meaning.

Example:

**F# F# F# F# → A below**

Because the listener has been living in “F# land,” the descending major-sixth move can feel like a major emotional release.

Depending on harmony and rhythm it may read as:
- tenderness
- hope
- wistfulness
- surrender
- falling safely into something warmer

---

# 7. Repeating an interval gesture

Repeating the same interval does something different from merely repeating a note.

- **Repeated ascending interval**
Can create:
- escalation
- insistence
- climbing
- ambition
- sequence
- increasing emotional pressure

- **Repeated descending interval**
Can create:
- settling
- inevitability
- lament
- cascading release
- collapse

- **Alternating up/down interval**
Can create:
- rocking
- dialogue
- indecision
- tenderness
- obsession
- playfulness

The interval’s core color remains, but repetition turns it into a **behavior**.

---

# 8. Chord-tone emotional roles

When a melody note sits over a chord, its scale degree against the chord root gives it another emotional identity.

These are broad tendencies.

- **Root / 1**
Home, identity, finality, certainty.

- **b2 / b9**
Pain, menace, extreme friction, exotic color.

- **2 / 9**
Openness, suspension, freshness, yearning without severe pain.

- **b3**
Minor identity, sadness, intimacy, darkness.

- **3**
Major identity, warmth, confidence, clarity.

- **4 / 11**
Suspension, nobility, questioning, openness.

- **#4 / #11**
Wonder, brightness with strangeness, magic, Lydian lift, alien radiance.

- **5**
Strength, stability, openness.

- **b6 / b13**
Dark beauty, altered color, tragedy, modal weight.

- **6 / 13**
Warmth, tenderness, richness, freedom.

- **b7**
Breadth, blues, rebellion, dominant energy, openness.

- **7 / maj7**
Yearning upward, sophistication, fragile tension, dreaminess.

- **Octave**
Identity reinforced and enlarged.

This is why **the same destination interval can feel different over a different chord**.

---

# 9. Bass intervals are not just “melody but lower”

The bass helps tell the ear what the harmony *is*.

That means interval motion in the bass affects the emotional world more strongly.

Broad tendencies:

- **Bass by semitone**
Sliding, ominous, sophisticated, inevitable.

- **Bass by whole step**
Connected progress, journey, walking movement.

- **Bass by 3rd**
Colorful, cinematic, mediant-like, emotionally surprising.

- **Bass by 4th or 5th**
Strong tonal direction, purpose, inevitability.

- **Bass by tritone**
Severe destabilization, remoteness, danger.

- **Bass by 6th or 7th**
Large harmonic displacement, cinematic scale, sudden perspective change.

- **Bass by octave**
Same identity, different weight.

---

# 10. Register changes the speaker

A melody is not emotionally identical in every octave.

- **Low register**
Feels:
- physical
- heavy
- primal
- ominous
- grounded
- powerful

- **Middle register**
Feels:
- human
- speakable
- lyrical
- intimate
- direct

- **High register**
Feels:
- exposed
- fragile
- radiant
- transcendent
- ecstatic
- distant

So an ascending major sixth into a high register can become much more heroic or transcendent than the same interval entirely within the middle register.

---

# 11. Articulation changes the intention

- **Legato**
Connection, tenderness, inevitability, flow.

- **Staccato**
Playfulness, precision, nervousness, aggression, wit.

- **Accent**
Importance, declaration, attack.

- **Marcato**
Heroism, authority, insistence.

- **Tremolo**
Instability, suspense, shimmering energy.

- **Portamento / slide**
Yearning, sensuality, grief, emotional exposure.

- **Pizzicato**
Lightness, stealth, comedy, delicacy, rhythmic clarity.

The interval supplies the shape.

The articulation tells the listener **how the character speaks the shape**.

---

# 12. Orchestration changes the identity of the same gesture

An ascending perfect fifth is not automatically “heroic.”

- **Horns + forte + octaves**
Heroic proclamation.

- **Solo violin + legato**
Yearning or romantic expansion.

- **Flute + pianissimo**
Innocence, air, distance.

- **Low brass + marcato**
Power, threat, military force.

- **Choir**
Sacredness, grandeur, transcendence.

- **Synth pad**
Dream, cosmic scale, suspension.

- **Distorted guitar**
Power, rebellion, aggression.

The gesture and orchestration multiply each other.

---

# 13. The most useful composing equation

For practical writing, think:

> **Emotional result = interval color + direction + tonal role + phrase role + metric weight + duration + register + repetition + harmony + articulation + orchestration**

You do not need to consciously calculate all ten every time.

Usually 2–4 of them dominate.

# 14. A practical melody-writing workflow

When following a chord progression, do **not** begin with:

> “What notes am I allowed to play in this bar?”

Begin with:

> **“What does this part of the sentence need to say?”**

Then:

- **1. Decide the phrase emotion**
Examples:
- continue
- question
- answer
- reach
- become heroic
- soften
- grieve
- open
- collapse
- return

- **2. Choose one structural destination note**
Often one important target per bar is enough to start.

- **3. Choose its interval from the previous important note**
Use this guide to decide the gesture.

Examples:

**Need forward motion?**  
Try a 2nd.

**Need emotional intimacy?**  
Try a 3rd.

**Need declaration?**  
Try a 4th.

**Need scale and power?**  
Try a 5th.

**Need soaring emotional expansion?**  
Try an ascending major 6th.

**Need impossible yearning?**  
Try a minor 6th or major 7th.

- **4. Check what the destination means over the chord**
Is it:
- root?
- 3rd?
- 5th?
- 7th?
- 9th?
- suspension?
- altered color?

Now you know both:

**horizontal meaning** + **vertical meaning**

- **5. Decide beat placement**
Strong beat = structural statement.  
Weak beat = motion/color.

- **6. Decide duration**
Short = passing.  
Long = emotionally evaluated.

- **7. Fill the connective notes**
Now add:
- passing notes
- neighbors
- repeated notes
- anticipation
- syncopation
- embellishment

These are the pronunciation of the sentence.

- **8. Decide whether the phrase closes or opens**
A phrase ending can:
- arrive
- suspend
- question
- fall
- soar
- refuse to resolve

That choice determines how the next musical sentence begins.

---

# 15. Structural notes vs. connective notes

Not every note in a melody carries equal emotional responsibility.

Think in two layers.

## Structural notes
These are the notes the listener remembers as the skeleton of the phrase.

They often:
- land on strong beats
- last longer
- sit at melodic peaks
- begin or end phrases
- recur
- form important interval gestures

## Connective notes
These explain how you travel between structural notes.

They include:
- passing tones
- neighbor tones
- runs
- ornaments
- approach notes
- rhythmic repetitions

You generally do **not** need to emotionally engineer every sixteenth note.

Engineer the important targets first.

---

# 16. Musical speech hierarchy

A useful hierarchy is:

- **Whole song**
The emotional story.

- **Section**
The paragraph.

- **Phrase**
The sentence.

- **Structural-note interval**
The important emotional verb.

- **Chord relationship**
The emotional context of that word.

- **Passing notes**
Pronunciation and connective grammar.

- **Rhythm**
Cadence, urgency, hesitation, emphasis.

- **Orchestration**
Who is speaking and with what emotional force.

This is much closer to how a composer can deliberately **speak in music** than treating melody as a bag of “legal scale notes.”

# 17. The F# → A example

This example shows why register and direction matter.

- **F# → A above**
Ascending minor 3rd.

Likely qualities:
- earnest
- determined
- emotional
- hopeful-through-pain
- vulnerable heroism

- **F# → A below**
Descending major 6th.

Likely qualities:
- tender
- releasing
- wistful
- hopeful
- affectionate
- emotionally expansive

If F# has been repeated for a long time, the effect becomes stronger because the ear has learned:

> **F# is where we have been living.**

The drop to A below can therefore feel like leaving a familiar emotional space and falling into something unexpectedly warm.

# 18. Quick emotional families

When you need a fast choice:

- **Stepwise family — m2 / M2**
**Speech and motion**

m2 = painful / urgent  
M2 = natural / continuing

- **Third family — m3 / M3**
**Human emotion**

m3 = intimate / wistful  
M3 = warm / confident

- **Fourth / fifth family**
**Structure and declaration**

P4 = noble / rising / questioning  
P5 = powerful / vast / certain

- **Tritone**
**Disruption**

Alien / unstable / transforming

- **Sixth family**
**Large emotion**

m6 = tragic yearning  
M6 = expansive warmth / soaring freedom

- **Seventh family**
**Distance and incompletion**

m7 = broad / rebellious / unresolved  
M7 = transcendent / fragile / almost impossible

- **Octave**
**Identity magnified**

Same self, larger scale.

---

# 19. Final rule of thumb

Do not ask only:

> **“What interval is this?”**

Ask:

> **“What is this interval doing?”**

Specifically:

- Where did I come from?
- Did I go up or down?
- What currently feels like home?
- Is this inside the sentence or between sentences?
- Did I land on a strong or weak beat?
- Is the note short or held?
- Have I repeated the starting pitch enough to establish a local center?
- What does the destination note mean against the chord?
- Is this happening in the bass?
- What register am I entering?
- What articulation and instrument are speaking it?
- Does the gesture resolve, suspend, or demand another answer?

That is where interval theory becomes **musical language**.
